FOB Price in Clothing Manufacturing: What Does It Include?

FOB means Free on Board. With a named port such as “FOB Xiamen,” the seller delivers the goods on board the buyer-nominated vessel and handles export clearance. Risk transfers when the goods are on board; the buyer then pays for main freight, optional cargo insurance, import clearance, duties and delivery from the destination port.

FOB is an Incoterms® rule for sea or inland waterway transport—not air freight. For air cargo or containerized goods handed to a carrier before vessel loading, FCA may be more appropriate. Before accepting a quote, compare the product specification, named port and excluded logistics costs with our clothing manufacturing cost guide and shipping guide.

Planning note: Incoterms allocate delivery tasks, costs and risk between seller and buyer; they do not replace a complete sales contract or define your garment specifications, payment terms or final landed cost.

1. The Definition: What Does FOB Actually Mean?

FOB stands for Free on Board. Under Incoterms® 2020, the seller delivers when the goods are placed on board the vessel nominated by the buyer at the named port of shipment. The seller completes export clearance; risk of loss or damage transfers to the buyer once the goods are on board.

FOB should identify the exact port, for example “FOB Xiamen, Incoterms® 2020.” It is intended for sea or inland waterway shipments. It should not be used merely as a synonym for a factory unit price.

2. What Is Included in an FOB Price Quote?

When a factory quotes “$8.50 FOB Xiamen,” separate the garment specification from the Incoterms cost allocation. Confirm that the commercial quote includes:

  • Garment specification: Agreed fabric, trims, construction and workmanship.
  • Branding and packaging: Only the labels, hangtags, poly bags and cartons written into the quotation.
  • Origin logistics: Transport to the named port and the seller’s export-clearance obligations.
  • On-board delivery: Costs and risk up to delivery on board the nominated vessel.

Normally excluded after FOB delivery: Main international freight, cargo insurance, destination charges, import duties and taxes, customs brokerage, and delivery to your warehouse.

3. FOB vs. EXW vs. FCA: Which Rule Fits?

Choose the rule according to where delivery occurs and how the goods travel:

  • EXW (Ex Works): The buyer takes on most transport and export arrangements from the seller’s premises.
  • FCA (Free Carrier): The seller delivers to the buyer’s carrier at the agreed place and completes export clearance. FCA can suit air freight, multimodal transport and container-terminal delivery.
  • FOB (Free on Board): The seller delivers on board the vessel at the named sea or river port. Use it when the contract genuinely calls for on-board vessel delivery.

4. FOB vs. CIF: Compare Cost and Control

Under CIF, the seller arranges and pays the cost of sea freight and minimum insurance to the named destination port, while risk still transfers at shipment under the rule. Under FOB, the buyer arranges the main freight after on-board delivery at the origin port.

  • FOB: More control over the freight forwarder, routing and quoted international transport cost.
  • CIF: The seller arranges freight and insurance to the named destination port, but the buyer should still confirm destination handling, import and delivery charges.

Compare both offers on total landed cost, not the garment unit price alone.

5. Why "FOB Xiamen" or "FOB Guangzhou" Matters

The port named after the "FOB" is critical. If your freight forwarder is based in Ningbo but your factory quotes "FOB Xiamen," you will have to pay for the trucking between those two cities. Always ensure your factory is quoting from the port nearest to their production facility to keep local costs as low as possible.

6. Key Questions to Ask Your Manufacturer About Pricing

Before accepting an FOB quote, ask:

  • Does the price include the agreed labels, packaging and export-grade cartons?
  • Which exact port and Incoterms® edition are named in the quotation?
  • Who books the vessel and what documents must each party provide?
  • What are the estimated carton dimensions, weight and ready date for the freight quote?
  • Which destination, import, duty and final-delivery costs remain outside the quote?

Frequently Asked Questions About FOB Clothing Prices

What does FOB mean in clothing manufacturing?

FOB means Free on Board. Under FOB with a named port, the seller delivers the goods on board the buyer-nominated vessel and completes export clearance. Risk then transfers to the buyer, who arranges the main freight, insurance if desired, import clearance, duties and destination delivery.

What is included in a FOB price?

A garment quotation may include fabric, labor, agreed labels and packaging, while FOB allocates origin transport, export clearance and on-board vessel delivery to the seller. Confirm every product and packaging item in writing because Incoterms do not define the garment specification.

Should I use FOB for air freight or container shipments?

FOB is intended for sea or inland waterway transport when delivery occurs on board a vessel. If goods are handed to a carrier at an air cargo terminal or container terminal before vessel loading, FCA is generally more appropriate.

What is the difference between FOB and CIF?

Under FOB, the buyer pays for the main international freight and insurance. Under CIF (Cost, Insurance, and Freight), the seller pays to get the goods all the way to the destination port.

Can I negotiate FOB prices?

Yes, FOB prices are highly negotiable based on order volume. A higher MOQ allows the factory to spread the fixed costs (like local trucking and export documents) over more units, lowering the price.
