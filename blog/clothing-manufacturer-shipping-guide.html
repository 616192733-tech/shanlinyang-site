GUIDE — LOGISTICS & SHIPPING

Shipping Clothing from China: Air, Sea, DDP and Landed Cost

Use courier for small urgent cartons, air freight for larger time-sensitive shipments, and sea freight when volume and cost matter more than speed. Compare each option using the final packed weight and dimensions, origin and destination charges, duties, customs clearance and last-mile delivery—not the freight rate alone.

This guide explains courier, air and sea options, DDP and FOB responsibilities, chargeable weight and realistic planning buffers. Before booking, ask for final carton data and compare the full landed cost with our FOB clothing price guide, manufacturing cost guide and production timeline guide.

Planning note: Transit times and charges are estimates. Route, season, carrier capacity, customs, duties, remote-area delivery and regulatory requirements can change the final schedule and cost.

1. Courier vs. Air Freight: Which Is Better?

International courier services such as DHL, FedEx or UPS can suit samples, small launches and urgent replenishment. Service scope varies: confirm whether the quote includes pickup, brokerage, duties, taxes and delivery to the exact address. A quoted transit time normally starts after carrier pickup and may exclude customs delays.

Air freight can become more economical for larger time-sensitive shipments, but airport handling, brokerage, customs and final-mile delivery may be separate. Compare total door-to-door cost and chargeable weight rather than assuming an arbitrary weight threshold.

2. Sea Freight: The High-Volume Choice

Sea freight can reduce the transport cost per unit for larger volumes, but garment count alone is not enough to choose it. Compare packed cubic volume, weight, LCL minimum charges, destination handling, customs and final delivery. Port-to-port transit may take roughly 4–6 weeks on many routes, with booking and destination processing adding more time.

  • LCL (Less than Container Load): Your goods share space in a container with other companies' products. You pay for the volume (CBM) you use.
  • FCL (Full Container Load): You rent the entire container. This is faster and more secure than LCL but only worth it for very large orders.

3. DDP (Delivered Duty Paid): Confirm the Details

Under DDP, the seller bears costs and risks to the named destination and handles import clearance, duties and applicable taxes. It is the Incoterms® rule with the seller’s highest level of responsibility. However, destination-country rules can make import registration or tax handling difficult for a foreign seller.

Do not rely on “DDP” alone. Confirm the exact named place, importer of record, duties and taxes, customs brokerage, destination handling, remote-area charges, delivery appointment and unloading responsibility. Ask whether any amount can be billed separately.

4. Understanding Chargeable and Volumetric Weight

Clothing can be light but bulky, so courier and air services may charge the higher of actual weight and volumetric weight. DHL Express commonly uses length × width × height in centimeters divided by 5,000, but divisors and density rules vary by carrier, service and transport mode.

Ask for the final number of cartons, outer dimensions and gross weight, then calculate using the chosen carrier’s current rules. Reduce empty space with appropriately sized cartons; only use compression or vacuum packing when it will not damage the fabric, trims, print or presentation.

5. Managing Lead Times and "Buffer Stock"

Port congestion, peak seasons, holidays, weather, inspections and customs can add days or weeks. Build a route-specific contingency instead of using one fixed buffer: confirm the factory ready date, booking cutoff, estimated departure and arrival, clearance allowance and final-mile appointment before setting the public launch date.

6. Confirm the Origin and First-Mile Plan

Shanlinyang ships from Xiamen, with access to port and air-cargo services. Ask which origin terminal, forwarder, pickup date and export documents apply to your order. First-mile proximity can simplify coordination, but it does not guarantee a fixed time saving because bookings, consolidation and carrier schedules still control departure.

Frequently Asked Questions About Shipping Clothing from China

How long does it take to ship clothing from China to the US?

Courier delivery may take roughly 3–7 business days after pickup, while sea freight often requires about 25–45 days port to port. Booking, export handling, customs, destination charges and final-mile delivery add time, so obtain a route-specific estimate.

What does DDP mean in shipping?

DDP means the seller bears costs and risks to the named destination and handles import clearance, duties and applicable taxes. Confirm the importer of record, named place, tax treatment and every excluded charge in writing.

Is sea freight worth it for small orders?

There is no universal garment-count threshold. Compare courier, air and LCL sea quotes using packed dimensions, weight, route, destination charges, customs and required delivery date.

How are shipping costs calculated?

Courier and air services often compare actual weight with volumetric weight and charge the higher figure. The divisor and rules vary, so confirm the current calculation with the selected carrier.
