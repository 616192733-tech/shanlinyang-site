Clothing Sample Approval Process: Fit, PPS and Bulk Release Checklist

PUBLISHED 2026-06-29 · UPDATED 2026-11-02 · SHANLINYANG APPAREL FACTORY TEAM · ~10 MIN READ

Approve a clothing sample only after the garment, measurement report and written specification agree on fit, materials, construction, branding and packaging. A clear sign-off record gives the factory one production standard and gives your quality team something objective to inspect against.

This guide focuses on reviewing and approving samples. If you are still preparing the first request, begin with how to order clothing samples from China. For the next stage, use the sample-to-bulk order guide.

Know which sample you are approving

Sample names vary between factories, so define the purpose rather than relying on the label alone.

Sample stageMain decisionWhat may still change
Prototype or development sampleDesign, proportion and construction feasibilityPattern, materials, trims and artwork may still be provisional
Fit sampleMeasurements, balance, comfort and movementColor, branding or final trims may not yet be approved
Size setGrading and fit across selected sizesFinal production details should be documented separately
Pre-production sample (PPS)Release the approved specification to bulk productionOnly written, controlled exceptions should remain
Top-of-production sampleConfirm early bulk output follows the approved standardCorrect production deviations before the run progresses

Use one approval checklist

Review the sample in a fixed order and record pass, revise or not applicable for every line. Do not rely on scattered messages across email and chat.

  • Measurements: measure every required point of measure, compare it with the specification and record the actual value and tolerance result.
  • Fit and movement: check balance, rise, armhole, neckline, hem, seam position and movement on the intended body or fit model.
  • Fabric and color: confirm composition, weight, stretch, recovery, hand feel, opacity and approved color reference.
  • Construction: inspect seam type, stitch consistency, reinforcement, edge finishing, symmetry and stress points.
  • Trims and branding: verify zippers, buttons, elastic, labels, artwork method, placement, size and color.
  • Performance: complete the agreed wash, shrinkage, colorfastness, pilling or stretch-recovery checks appropriate to the product.
  • Packaging: approve folding, hangtags, barcodes, size stickers, polybags, carton marks and assortment instructions.

Measure before the fit session

Lay the garment flat using the measurement method stated in the tech pack. Record target, tolerance and actual measurement rather than writing only “pass” or “fail.” If the measurement method is unclear, align it with the factory before judging the result; different starting points or levels of fabric tension can produce different numbers.

Then conduct the fit session. Use photos from the front, back and side, plus close-ups of any issue. Describe a change as a measurable action—for example, “reduce the front neck width by 1 cm” or “add 1.5 cm to the back rise”—and state which related measurements must remain unchanged.

Decide whether a revised sample is required

Approving with comments may be reasonable for a minor, measurable correction such as a label position or thread color. Request another sample when a change affects the pattern, fit, fabric performance, logo execution, safety, regulatory information or several construction details at once. The cost of one additional round is usually easier to control than a disputed bulk order.

Never approve an unspecified change verbally. Put each revision into one numbered comment sheet, attach annotated photos and ask the factory to reply against the same list.

Build the pre-production approval record

The PPS should reflect the production specification or clearly identify any approved exception. Before release, assemble one approval pack containing:

  1. the dated specification and measurement chart version;
  2. the approved sample photos and comment sheet;
  3. fabric, color and trim references;
  4. approved artwork and label files with placement measurements;
  5. test results and care instructions;
  6. packaging and carton requirements; and
  7. the purchase order, quantity, size/color breakdown and inspection plan.

Both brand and factory should be able to identify the same controlling version. Keep a physical approved sample when practical, but do not let the garment replace the written specification: samples can be lost, stretched or handled differently over time.

Release bulk production in writing

Send a short release statement that names the approved sample and document versions, lists any remaining conditions and confirms whether production may begin. Ask the factory to acknowledge the release. If an important material, trim or construction detail changes later, reopen approval for that point instead of assuming the old sign-off still applies.

Align this approval record with your quality-control and inspection process. For a project-specific review, request a sample and PPS quotation with your tech pack, target quantity and destination country.

Clothing Sample Approval FAQ

What must be approved before bulk production?

Approve measurements and tolerances, fit, construction, final fabric and color, trims, labels, artwork placement, wash-test result and packaging. Record any exceptions and the exact controlling version.

What is the difference between a fit sample and a PPS?

A fit sample verifies measurements, balance and movement. A PPS should represent the approved production specification, including final or explicitly approved materials, trims, branding and construction.

Can I approve a sample with comments?

Minor, measurable, low-risk changes can sometimes be approved with comments. Request a revised sample when the change affects fit, pattern, fabric performance, branding, safety or several construction details.

What approval records should I keep?

Keep dated photos, the signed comment sheet, measurements, artwork, material references, test notes, packaging instructions and the approved specification version.

Should every size and color be sampled?

The plan depends on risk and order size. Consider a size set or added color samples when grading, dyes, prints, trims or fit vary materially.
